FAQs

Questions parents and schools often ask

If your question isn't here, get in touch and I'll be happy to help.

Does my child need to be good at art?

Not at all. Art therapy isn't an art lesson and nothing is judged or marked. Children use art materials, play and talking to express themselves in whatever way feels right to them.

What happens in a session?

Each session takes place in the same room, at the same time each week. A range of art materials and toys is available, and the child chooses what to do. Some children make art the whole time, some play, some talk. I follow their lead and help them make sense of what comes up.

How long are sessions, and how often?

Individual sessions are 50 minutes, once a week, held in school in a consistent room. That regular rhythm helps children build trust.

Before sessions start, there's a 60 minute assessment with parents or guardians. After 6 weeks, and then every half term, we meet for a 60 minute review of your child's progress.

My child doesn't like talking about feelings. Can art therapy still help?

Yes. That's often exactly why art therapy is suggested. Making something can be a way of expressing feelings that are hard to put into words, and children don't have to talk unless they want to.

How long does art therapy last?

It depends on the child. Some children come for a term, others for a year or more. We review progress regularly and plan the ending carefully, because a good ending matters too.

Will you tell me what my child says or makes?

What happens in sessions is kept confidential, so that children feel safe to explore. I share general themes and progress with parents and carers and, where relevant, with school staff.

The exception is safeguarding. If I'm worried that a child is at risk of harm, I have to share that with the right people.

The assessment is £90. Individual art therapy sessions are £85 each. Regular and end reviews are £90 each.

Do you work with schools?

Yes. I work with schools to offer regular sessions for pupils on site. Find out how it works for schools.
